Work and Energy

  • Work is done only when a force produces displacement in its direction. A force acting without movement does no work.
  • Energy is the ability of a body to do work. Energy is the cause; work is the effect.
  • The SI unit of both work and energy is the joule (J). One joule equals one newton metre.

Mechanical Energy

  • Kinetic energy is energy due to motion. It increases with mass and with the square of speed.
  • Potential energy is stored due to position or change of shape. Gravitational PE increases with mass and height.
  • Stretched bands, drawn bows and compressed springs store elastic potential energy.

Forms and Transformations

  • Common forms include heat, light, sound and electrical energy; stored forms include muscular, chemical, magnetic and nuclear energy.
  • Examples: fan: electrical → mechanical; microphone: sound → electrical; bulb: electrical → light + heat; solar cell: light → electrical.
  • Hydroelectricity: potential → kinetic → mechanical → electrical. Photosynthesis: light → chemical.

Conservation of Energy

  • Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed or transferred; total energy remains constant.
  • Pendulum extremes have maximum PE and zero KE; the mean position has maximum KE and minimum PE.
  • Friction changes useful mechanical energy into heat and sound. This is dissipation, not destruction of energy.
