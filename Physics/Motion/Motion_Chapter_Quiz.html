01 · Chapter map

What you should know before starting

Rest, motion and reference

Motion is a change in position with respect to surroundings and time. Rest and motion are relative: the same object can be at rest for one observer and moving for another.

Translatory motion

All parts travel the same distance in the same time. A straight path is rectilinear; a curved path is curvilinear.

Circular and rotatory

In circular motion an object goes around a fixed point at constant radius. In rotatory motion a body spins about its own fixed axis.

Oscillatory and vibratory

Oscillatory motion is to-and-fro about a mean position. Vibratory motion is rapid, small-amplitude to-and-fro motion of part of a body.

Repeating and irregular motion

Periodic motion repeats after a fixed interval. Non-periodic motion does not. Random motion has no fixed path and changes direction suddenly.

Multiple and rolling motion

An object can have more than one motion. Rolling combines rotatory and translatory motion; Earth rotates on its axis while revolving around the Sun.

Distance and displacement

Distance is total path length and is a scalar. Displacement is the shortest straight-line change from start to finish, with direction, so it is a vector.

Uniform and non-uniform

Uniform motion covers equal distances in equal time intervals. Non-uniform motion covers unequal distances in equal intervals or equal distances in unequal intervals.

Graphs, speed and velocity

A straight sloping distance-time line means constant speed; a horizontal line means rest. Speed uses distance; velocity uses displacement and direction.

Simple pendulum

A bob suspended by a light string oscillates about its mean position. Its time period is the time taken for one complete oscillation.

Mass and weight

Mass is the amount of matter and remains constant. Weight is the gravitational force on that mass, so it changes when gravity changes.

Exam habits

  • Convert all quantities to compatible units.
  • Write the formula before substitution.
  • Always include the final unit and direction when needed.
02 · Formula bank

Important formulas and quick rules

Speed
Speed = distance ÷ time
SI unit: metre per second (m/s)
Distance
Distance = speed × time
Use matching units throughout.
Time
Time = distance ÷ speed
SI unit: second (s)
Average speed
Total distance ÷ total time
Do not simply average separate speeds.
Velocity
Velocity = displacement ÷ time
State both magnitude and direction.
Convert km/h to m/s
Multiply by 5/18
Example: 108 km/h = 30 m/s
Convert m/s to km/h
Multiply by 18/5
Equivalent to multiplying by 3.6.
Pendulum time period
T = total time ÷ oscillations
Unit: second (s)
Distance-time graph
Speed = change in distance ÷ change in time
A steeper line represents greater speed.
Distance rule
Distance ≥ displacement
They are equal only for a straight, one-direction journey.
Weight
Weight = mass × gravity
Weight on the Moon is about 1/6 of Earth weight.
One complete lap
Displacement = 0
Start and final positions are the same.
